## Fix before presenting these as teaching targets

1. **Spinal nerve target includes a sensory-only root segment (line 107).** The path `M379 177Q401 200 392 255H433` highlights the continuation from the dorsal root ganglion down to the motor-root junction as well as the mixed nerve after the junction. The proximal curve is still dorsal-root/sensory territory. Move `M379 177Q401 200 392 255` into the dorsal-root target, and make spinal-nerve only `M392 255H433`. The distinction is exactly what the question's note is teaching, so the highlight must respect it. Supporting university source: https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/schemes/back_tables.html (web ref `turn3922346search0`).

2. **False connections from unmarked line crossings in the brachial plexus (long thoracic item, line 32).** Its long vertical segment at x85 crosses the drawn C8 and T1 ramus paths at roughly y295 and y355. All are rendered as continuous same-style nerve lines, so a learner can infer C8/T1 contributions. The stated C5-C7 anatomy is correct; the geometry is misleading. Route the descending long thoracic line to the left of all five root paths, then feed only C5-C7 into it. Alternatively use explicit bridge gaps and junction dots, consistently across the entire connection schematic. The C7 contribution also forms a redundant little loop with the continuous x85 stem; simplify to one genuine junction per contributing ramus. Source: https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/musculoskeletal_system/axilla_tables.html (`turn3922346search1`), or https://medicine.uams.edu/neuroscience/education/medical-school-courses/human-structure-module/anatomy-tables/nerve-tables/nerves-of-the-upper-limb/ (`turn3922346search8`).

3. **False arterial connections at 2D crossings (lines 78, 82, 84).** The splenic branch crosses the aortic main line above the celiac node. The SMA passes through the horizontal renal arterial path around x245,y253. Without bridge gaps or an explicit crossing convention, both crossings look like anastomotic junctions, teaching the wrong topology. Preserve true branch junctions and break/reroute the unrelated crossing paths. The written branching statements are otherwise appropriate conventional anatomy. Source: https://medicine.uams.edu/neuroscience/education/medical-school-courses/human-structure-module/anatomy-tables/artery-tables/arteries-of-the-abdomen/ (`turn8018850view3`).

4. **Missing gray commissure around the central canal (lines 92-103).** The dorsal/ventral horn paths stop near x205 on the left and x225 on the right; no gray bridge surrounds the canal at x215,y270. The central canal consequently floats in a gap despite its note correctly stating that it lies in the gray commissure. Add a non-target gray-matter connector enclosing the central-canal region and continuous with both horns. Keep the anterior white commissure ventral to that connector. Source: https://nba.uth.tmc.edu/neuroscience/m/s2/chapter03.html (`turn8018850view4`, find ref `turn9174121view3`).

5. **Cribriform foramina / plate are not interchangeable target names (line 115).** The target is called `Cribriform foramina`, accepts `Cribriform plate` as an alias, and highlights one solid midline rectangle. The perforations and the bone plate are distinct anatomical structures. Either rename the target to `Cribriform plate`, with paired plate areas flanking a small crista galli guide, or draw multiple discrete openings in those paired areas and retain `Cribriform foramina` without treating the whole plate as a synonym. Source: https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/anatomytables/bones_head_neck.html (`turn8018850view5`, lines 12-14).

## Checked and reasonable as explicitly labelled schematics

- Brachial plexus root/trunk/division/cord logic is conventional: C5+C6 superior, C7 middle, C8+T1 inferior; superior and middle anterior divisions join lateral cord; inferior anterior continues medial; all posterior divisions join posterior cord. Terminal branch relationships and the two median roots are correct. The median-root paths do cross other branch curves, so use the same crossing/junction convention there.
- Cerebral ring is connected through A1, ACom, PCom and proximal PCA segments, with the internal carotid junctions. **MCA is correctly an outflow branch, not a circle segment.** Basilar/vertebral continuity and usual SCA/AICA/PICA parent vessels are appropriate. The paired grouping and normal-variant caveats are useful. Do not label the whole ICA/ACA/PCA highlighted length as 'a ring segment' if distal outflow portions are also highlighted.
- Abdominal aortic branch order, celiac triad, hepatic/GDA relation, broad SMA/IMA territories and iliac continuities are reasonable. The view's subject-right/left labels put liver-directed branches on the correct screen side. This remains a branch diagram, not an organ-location atlas.
- Cord dorsal-at-top orientation is explicit. Gracile medial to cuneate, lateral corticospinal dorsolateral to anterolateral spinothalamic positions, and a thoracic lateral horn are reasonable approximate placements. 'Upper thoracic' makes the cuneate component appropriate. The anterior white commissure note and tract crossing statements are suitable bounded teaching descriptions.
- Optic canal, superior orbital fissure, rotundum, ovale, spinosum, internal acoustic meatus, jugular foramen, hypoglossal canal and foramen magnum contents stated here are reasonable. Several notes intentionally give principal rather than exhaustive contents; do not market them as full content lists.

## Coverage limits

These six schematics are a useful advanced addition but do not establish complete anatomy coverage. Examples still absent from the particular plates: many collateral plexus branches, complete regional vascular branching, spinal rami/meningeal context, additional skull openings and vessel/sympathetic contents. Those are coverage tasks, not reasons to mislabel current drawings. Actual licensed atlas views and independently reviewed region diagrams are still needed for a complete atlas claim.
